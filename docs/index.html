Overview

What this package contains

Healthcare AI systems are increasingly shared as open models. The EU AI Act classifies many as high-risk, demanding transparency about trustworthiness, bias, and interpretability. This package reproduces every step from candidate collection to the final regression tables.

Claim-vs-evidence coding

3 dimensions — trust/safety, bias/fairness, interpretability — coded as claim (yes/no) + evidence (yes/partial/no) per model card.

Self-collected control group

500 general-purpose (non-health) models, identical pipeline — enables a direct, internally-valid domain comparison instead of borrowing external baselines.

3-run reliability check

Every card classified 3× independently, aggregated by majority vote. Two conclusions changed once reliability was taken seriously — reported transparently.

Results

Three research questions

All numbers use majority-voted labels across the 3 classification runs. Popularity (downloads) is not tested as a standalone RQ — it's carried as a control variable inside RQ3's regression.

Any-claim vs. disclaimer rate, pre/post EU AI Act (2024‑08‑01)
Disclaimer ↑ significant · χ²=4.80, p=0.0285

Any-claim rate rose from 6.3% (N=80, pre) to 10.2% (N=420, post) — not significant (χ²=0.815, p=0.367). Clinical-use disclaimers rose from 8.8% to 19.8% — significant.

Finding: regulation altered what developers write without affecting the part that would benefit a downstream user — a compliance-by-disclaimer pattern. Robust to the reliability check.

Organization- vs. individual-authored models
Not significant · χ²=1.940, p=0.164

Organizations claim more in absolute terms (12.8% vs. 8.3%), but the gap is not statistically significant. Gap score doesn't differ either (Mann-Whitney p=0.766) — the raw difference looks like classification-noise.

What does hold up: individual authors disclaim clinical use 3× more often than organizations (22.8% vs. 6.7%).

Multivariate logistic regression — odds ratios (N=1,000)
Marginal · OR=0.70, 95% CI [0.47, 1.05], p=0.082

Unadjusted: healthcare any-claim 9.6% vs. control 13.0% (χ²=2.554, p=0.110). Controlling for popularity and authorship, healthcare shows ~30% lower odds of making any documentation claim than the control group.

Just short of conventional significance — reported as suggestive, not confirmatory. Popularity itself is not a significant predictor here either.

Reliability

Fleiss' κ per field

Each card classified 3× independently by the same LLM/prompt. Agreement measured by raw % agreement and Fleiss' κ — not agreement with human ground truth (see Threats).

Two rare-category fields (bias_claim κ=0.218, bias_evidence κ=0.331) look unstable but have ≥98.6% raw agreement — a near-zero-prevalence statistical artifact, not poor classification.

Transparency

Threats to validity

Disclosed in full — including the parts that don't flatter the study.

!

No manual (human) ground-truth validation of the LLM classification, for either dataset — the 3-run check measures agreement between LLM passes, not agreement with humans.

!

Two rare-category fields (bias_claim, bias_evidence) show unstable Fleiss' κ (0.22–0.33) despite ≥98.6% raw agreement — a near-zero-prevalence artifact.

!

500-model samples are random, not stratified by sub-domain/modality; control group drawn from a recency-sorted pool of 20,000, not a uniform sample of the whole Hub.

!

Tag-based retrieval may miss relevant models or include borderline/irrelevant ones (quantization mirrors, placeholder cards).

!

Card text truncated to 3,000 characters — may miss evidence located later in very long cards.

!

Control group's "high-stakes-use disclaimer" field generalizes the healthcare "clinical-use disclaimer" field — not perfectly semantically equivalent.

!

RQ3's domain effect (p=0.082) is suggestive, not confirmatory; tests were not corrected for multiple comparisons, so this should be read with caution.

!

Generation parameters (temperature, top-p) were not controlled — accessed via agentic orchestration layer, not direct parameterized API — unknown/undisclosed by the harness.
